# Stage 2 baseline: what the merged skill knows, topic by topic

Mapped on 2026-09-28 after the product-ui-mode and greenfield-discovery merge, before any stage-2 finding reached the skill. Three read-only agents classified 48 topics from the stage-2 brief against `skills/website-redesign/` (SKILL.md, references, templates, script headers), quoting the most specific line the skill has on each. The full records, with the verbatim quote and the risk in a real run, are in `00-baseline.json`.

**Levels.** *known-well*: specific, correct, actionable. *shallow*: some substance, not enough to act. *skipped*: absent. *recommended-untested*: recommended with no evidence it was tried. *generic*: advice without decisions, thresholds or checks. *principle-without-implementation*: right idea, no how.

**Result:** 4 skipped, 4 principle-without-implementation, 1 recommended-untested, 1 generic, 28 shallow, 10 known-well.

**Where the skill is strong already** (do not duplicate): motion judgement and library choice, critique method and evidence rules, avoiding technology-first choices on ordinary pages, typography (Latin, Arabic numerals), colour and token architecture, the WCAG 2.2 baseline for conventional UI, and generic-AI-tell avoidance. The weak half is everything that makes a *rich* experience: the game-UI canon, sprites, sound, rendering choice, editors and history, degradation tiers, and the checks (width sweeps, content stress, motion verification) that would catch what a run misses.

**The one-sentence reading.** The skill knows how to stop a signature experience being shrunk into a form, and how to make a restrained product calm and correct; it does not yet know how to *build* the game-like thing that won at Cake Junction, how to choose the cheapest medium that carries it, or how to prove it works across widths, content and devices.
